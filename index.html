# vision

In the future, we won't just evolve genomes. We'll design them.

I started my career modeling how genomes are regulated, then spent years helping build synthetic ones: a tRNA neochromosome and debugged synthetic yeast chromosomes, alongside the Steinmetz, Boeke, and Cai labs.

That work taught me that genome design is still slow, imprecise, and unpredictable. Genomes are massively complex, evolved systems that carry the convoluted logic of their history, which is more than anyone can hold in their head. With AI, we can finally begin to understand them at scale, in context, and on their own terms.

But first-principles reasoning only goes so far with evolved systems, even for a model, so designs have to be tested. I firmly believe progress in biology with AI depends on tight lab-in-the-loop integration: fast design–build–test–learn (DBTL) cycles, where every experiment sharpens the next design.

That's what I'm building toward: agentic systems that design, simulate, and test biology fast enough that building a genome from scratch stops being a moonshot and starts becoming a roadmap.

cat about.md

I'm a scientist-turned-engineer working at the intersection of synthetic biology, genomics, and agentic AI.

After a Ph.D. in systems biology at the University of Washington and five years at EMBL unpacking how genomes are organized and regulated, I moved into industry to build the computational tools that make biological design faster — first statistical and machine-learning models at Inscripta and Ginkgo Bioworks, and now AI agents at Generate Biomedicines. I'm based in Albuquerque, NM, and work remotely with teams around the world.
